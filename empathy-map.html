Empathy Map: CEO of Meridian Retail Group

Understanding the pressures, perceptions, and contradictions facing the CEO as Meridian navigates omnichannel transformation.

Says

  • Strategic urgency: "Without reinvention, we'll lose an entire generation of customers."
  • Customer experience: "Our customers still value us, but we are no longer meeting their expectations."
  • Competition: "Digital-native players are setting the standard, and we are being compared against them every day."
  • Technology: "We have invested in tools, but we still do not have clear insight."
  • Operations: "We cannot afford stockouts on bestsellers while carrying excess inventory elsewhere."
  • Employees: "Our associates are the face of the brand, but we are not enabling them properly."
  • Leadership: "We need a transformation that delivers results, not another isolated initiative."

Thinks

  • Business survival: "Is this a temporary downturn, or are we becoming structurally irrelevant?"
  • Customer loyalty: "Have we lost the emotional connection customers once had with Meridian?"
  • Data confidence: "Can I trust the numbers I am getting from different departments?"
  • Execution risk: "Even if we know what to fix, can this organization execute fast enough?"
  • Transformation fatigue: "Have we overwhelmed teams with too many tools and change programs?"
  • Board pressure: "How do I convince the board this reinvention will produce measurable returns?"
  • Brand identity: "How do we modernize without losing the reliability and trust Meridian was built on?"
  • Talent risk: "If employees are disengaged, how can they deliver a better customer experience?"

Feels

  • Pressure: revenue, loyalty, market share, and brand perception are all declining at once.
  • Frustration: many tools and teams, but they are not creating clear business insight.
  • Anxiety: competitors are moving faster and customer expectations are changing quickly.
  • Accountability: ultimately responsible for failures across CX, operations, people, and technology.
  • Impatience: the organization is diagnosing problems too slowly while the market moves quickly.
  • Concern for employees: associates expected to deliver modern omnichannel service without enough support.
  • Fear of irrelevance: the biggest fear is Meridian becoming outdated in customers' minds.
  • Determination: knows reinvention is necessary and wants a credible path forward.

Sees

  • Online conversion falling from 2.8% to 1.4% — digital channels underperforming at a critical time.
  • Negative social sentiment — the brand is losing emotional goodwill.
  • Returns increasing by 36% — weak product info, expectation-setting, and customer confidence.
  • 17% of top-selling items frequently out of stock — losing sales on wanted products.
  • Overstock in slow-moving categories — capital trapped in the wrong inventory.
  • Employee turnover at 38% — the frontline experience is unstable.
  • Fragmented customer and loyalty data — can't understand customer value or repeat behavior.
  • Competitors offer personalization, virtual try-on, fast shipping, flexible returns — a newer standard.

Hears

  • Customers: "Product information is unclear." "The experience feels inconsistent." "Other retailers are easier."
  • Store associates: "The systems are confusing." "We are expected to do too much." "Policies are complicated."
  • Store managers: "We cannot keep good people." "Scheduling is difficult." "Training is not enough."
  • Merchants: "We cannot plan properly because the data is outdated."
  • Supply chain: "Inventory and warehouse processes are inconsistent."
  • IT: "Too many tools but not enough integration or insight."
  • Marketing: "We cannot personalize effectively because customer data is fragmented."
  • Board / investors: "What is the turnaround plan? How soon will growth and profitability improve?"
  • Competitors / market: "Speed, personalization, and convenience are now basic expectations."

Does

  • Pushes for reinvention — current model isn't keeping up with customer expectations.
  • Reviews sales, conversion, loyalty, and operational KPIs — to see where performance is breaking down.
  • Challenges functional leaders — wants accountability across stores, eCommerce, supply chain, HR, IT, marketing.
  • Seeks external advisory support — needs an objective view and a practical transformation roadmap.
  • Pursues quick wins plus long-term transformation — needs momentum while solving structural issues.
  • Communicates urgency internally — change is not optional.
  • Balances investment decisions — technology vs. people vs. supply chain vs. CX vs. brand.

Where the Shoe Bites

Core pain: The CEO is accountable for an entire omnichannel business but does not have a connected, reliable system to understand, control, or improve it fast enough.

Five Contradictions

  1. Accountability without visibility — "I am being asked to make enterprise-level decisions with disconnected and outdated information."
  2. Modernization without disruption — "I need to transform the plane while flying it."
  3. A trusted brand becoming an outdated brand — "The brand promise that made us successful may no longer be enough."
  4. Employees are both the solution and the bottleneck — "I need my people to create a better experience, but we have not created a better experience for them."
  5. Too many tools, not enough intelligence — "We have spent money on technology, but we have not converted it into insight, speed, or customer value."
